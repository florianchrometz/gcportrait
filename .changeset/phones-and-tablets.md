The app now works on phones and tablets as well as on desktops, with every control at every width. Narrower than a desktop window, the top bar's controls fold behind two buttons: View holds the lens, focus, filters, detail and the search, and More the pages, the Snapshot menu, Saved views, Copy link, Export, the theme and the account. A tablet keeps the bar's two rows; a phone has one, with a breadcrumb that rests at the level you are on and swipes sideways to the levels above it. The details open as a panel over the diagram's right side on a tablet and as a sheet over its lower half on a phone, which you drag up to the full height and back, or down to close it; their content scrolls inside the panel or sheet, and the "N of M shown" bar moves clear of it. The timeline, the pages and the dialogs wrap to fit: a table too wide for its page scrolls sideways in its own box, and on a phone the Permissions page lists what each mode needs under a heading per mode, each command as wide as the page. On a phone or a tablet every button, link and field is at least 44 × 44 pixels, except a link inside a sentence and a field inside a line of a command, no control is out of sight when it has keyboard focus, the bars keep clear of a phone's notch and rounded corners, and on the diagram one finger pans, two fingers pinch to zoom and a tap selects. A node's menu has no touch gesture, and drill-in none of its own (a double tap drills in only where the browser treats it as a double-click, as Chrome does): the details carry both actions. The desktop layout is unchanged, but for the Permissions page, which no longer scrolls past its footer in a short window. The website says the app works on every screen.
